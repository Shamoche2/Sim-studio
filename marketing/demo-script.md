# Demo script: showing Sim Studio to teachers (10–30 minutes)

**Bring:** a laptop or phone with the site open (or the offline zip if there's no internet), and printed flyers.

## 1. Start with their problem (1 minute)
Ask: *"Which topics are hardest for your students to picture?"*
Typical answers: electricity, waves, moles, electrolysis, genetics, osmosis. Pick their answer for the next step.

## 2. Show one simulation from their topic (3 minutes)
1. Type the topic in the search box and press **Enter**.
2. Read the one-line introduction aloud.
3. Move **one slider** and ask them to predict what will happen before you move it again.
4. Point to the live numbers and the **"Key idea"** box: "This is the exam point."
5. Show the **"Try this"** questions: "These are ready-made class questions."

## 3. Show it fits their syllabus (1 minute)
Choose **WAEC (WASSCE)** or **Cambridge IGCSE** in the *Syllabus* menu. Scroll through the topics: "Every simulation is listed under your syllabus topic."

## 4. Let them try (3 minutes)
Hand over the device, or ask them to open the link on their own phone. Let them explore. Don't talk.

## 5. Show the practical bits (2 minutes)
- **Full screen** button: for the projector.
- **Share link:** copy the address of a simulation and send it to students.
- **Download for offline use:** for labs with no internet. Copy to a USB stick.

## 6. Ask for a small commitment (1 minute)
*"Would you try one simulation in a lesson this week? I'll check in on Friday to hear how it went."*
Get their WhatsApp number. Leave a flyer.

## 7. Follow up (later that week)
Message them: *"How did the lesson go? What did students think? Anything missing or wrong?"*
If it went well, ask: *"Could I quote you on the website?"* (name, school, one sentence).

---

### Answers to common questions
- **Is it free?** Yes.
- **Does it need internet?** No. Download it once and it works offline.
- **Does it work on phones?** Yes, any modern phone browser.
- **Is it accurate?** It uses standard textbook science, and each page says what is simplified. If you spot a mistake, use the feedback link and it will be fixed.
- **Who made it?** You, a local developer, building it for teachers. Be proud of that!
